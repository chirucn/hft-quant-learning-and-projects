1. What is Sharpe Ratio?
    The Sharpe Ratio measures the risk-adjusted return of a trading strategy.
                Sharpe Ratio=(Rp-Rf)/𝜎p
        Interpretation:
            Sharpe > 1 → good
            Sharpe > 2 → very good
            Sharpe > 3 → excellent
        A higher Sharpe ratio means the strategy is earning more return per unit of risk, not just higher raw returns.

2. Why Drawdown Matters More Than Returns
    Drawdown is the percentage decline from a peak in equity to a trough.
        Reasons drawdown is more important than returns:
            Large drawdowns are psychologically hard to tolerate
            A 50% drawdown needs a 100% return just to break even
            Funds, prop desks, and investors care more about capital preservation
            High returns with deep drawdowns are often not scalable or survivable

Key insight:
A strategy that grows slower but avoids large drawdowns is often superior to a high-return but volatile strategy.

3. Difference Between CAGR and Total Return
# Total Return
   Total Return=(Final Value-Initial Value)/Initial Value
    > Ignores time
    > Misleading for strategies with different durations

# CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)
    CAGR=(Final Value/Initial Value)**(1/n)−1
    > Accounts for compounding
    > Allows fair comparison between strategies
    > Industry standard metric

Example:
A 100% return over 10 years is far worse than a 100% return over 2 years — CAGR captures this, total return does not.
